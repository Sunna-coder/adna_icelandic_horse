# Ethics Self-Assessment, Animal (Archaeological) Remains

*Drafted by adapting the human-remains ethics language from Hannes Schroeder's own MSCA/Marie Curie applications (ROOTS 2008, CARIB 2013, both in `background/MSCAs/`), which use a consistent structure: archaeological material >100 years old doesn't require formal ethical review; authorization instead comes from the holding institution/heritage authority; respectful treatment and return of unsampled material. Adapted below for animal (horse) rather than human remains, the main change is removing anything specific to human dignity/descendant communities, and explicitly clarifying this is not live-animal research, which is a different EU ethics category.*

*Intended for both the Part A Ethics Self-Assessment (two free-text fields) and Part B-2 Section 6 ("Additional Ethics Information").*

---

## Ethical dimension of the objectives, methodology and likely impact

The objective of this project is to generate and analyse ancient DNA from archaeological horse remains excavated in Iceland, spanning the settlement period (ca. 870 CE) to the late 19th century. The methodology involves destructive sampling of skeletal material (teeth and bone) held in the collection of the National Museum of Iceland, which results in the loss of a small quantity of material from each specimen analysed.

This project does **not** involve live animals, animal experimentation, or animal welfare considerations in the sense addressed by EU animal research legislation (e.g. Directive 2010/63/EU). All specimens are archaeological remains from animals that died thirty to over a thousand years ago, held in a museum collection prior to and independent of this project.

## Compliance with ethical principles and relevant legislation

As the material analysed is archaeological and consists of non-human (equine) remains, it falls outside the scope of both human-tissue legislation and live-animal research/welfare legislation. No formal ethical review is therefore required under EU or Icelandic law for this category of material.

Instead, access and sampling are governed by the authorisation of the holding institution: the National Museum of Iceland (Þjóðminjasafnið) has already granted permission covering all ancient Icelandic horse specimens relevant to this project. Copies of these permits can be provided on request.

All specimens will be treated with appropriate scientific and curatorial care throughout sampling, extraction, and analysis. Any unsampled material, and any remaining bone/tooth powder not consumed during extraction, will be returned to the National Museum of Iceland following completion of laboratory work, consistent with standard practice for museum-held archaeological collections.

## For Section 6 (Additional Ethics Information), condensed version

No formal ethical review is necessary. This project analyses ancient DNA from archaeological horse (non-human) remains, which is not subject to human-tissue legislation and does not constitute live-animal research under EU animal welfare legislation (e.g. Directive 2010/63/EU). All sampling is carried out under written authorisation from the National Museum of Iceland (Þjóðminjasafnið), the holding institution; copies of permits will be provided upon request. Unsampled material is returned to the Museum following completion of laboratory work.

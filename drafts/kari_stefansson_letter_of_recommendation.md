**Letter of Recommendation**

**[Date]**

To the Icelandic Research Fund,

I am writing in strong support of the application by Dr Sigríður Sunna Ebenesersdóttir for a Postdoctoral Fellowship for her project "From settlers to Icelanders: the genomic legacy of Iceland's founding population."

I have known Dr Ebenesersdóttir since she first came to deCODE Genetics as a master's student under Prof. Agnar Helgason, and I followed her development closely through her subsequent years as a Research Scientist in our Department of Anthropology (2013–2026), both as CEO of the company and as a population geneticist myself. I am now pleased that she continues to have access to our data and infrastructure as a Visiting Scientist, in support of her transition to an independent academic career at the University of Iceland.

Dr Ebenesersdóttir's scientific record speaks for itself. She was first author on Ebenesersdóttir et al. (2018, *Science*), the first genome-wide ancient DNA study of Iceland, for which she led both the laboratory work and the genomic analysis herself, generating the underlying data rather than simply analysing material produced by others. She is also co-first author on Gopalakrishnan, Ebenesersdóttir et al. (2022, *Current Biology*) on the genomic legacy of the second plague pandemic, and on a further plague pandemic study currently submitted to *Nature* (editorial decision on review still pending). In total she has authored or co-authored 13 papers in peer-reviewed journals, including *Science*, *Nature*, *Cell* (twice), and *Nature Genetics* (twice), with further manuscripts submitted or in preprint.

Beyond her publication record, Dr Ebenesersdóttir has made a lasting technical contribution to our organisation: she co-developed deCODE's population genomics pipeline specifically built for low-depth ancient samples, encompassing both the mapping pipeline and the GLIMPSE2-based imputation pipeline, and has been responsible for running and maintaining both. She also personally established and has operated our dedicated ancient DNA clean-room laboratory. The dataset underlying her proposed Rannís project, 201 ancient Icelandic genomes, was generated under her direct leadership.

Dr Ebenesersdóttir has also shown a genuine ability to communicate science to the public, something I have witnessed firsthand. I recall in particular the public conference we held at deCODE Genetics in June 2018, where she reported our new findings on the founding of the Icelandic population and its subsequent evolution based on ancient DNA, and the press conference that December, where she reported the results of our analyses on a skull discovered under the floorboards in the attic of the Prime Minister's Residence. Both received considerable public attention, and she handled them with the same clarity and rigour she brings to her scientific work.

In my years working with researchers at deCODE, I would place Dr Ebenesersdóttir among the strongest at her career stage: she combines hands-on technical capability, from sample to sequence to analysis, with the scientific judgement to lead a project of this scale independently. Her position at deCODE came to an end in 2026 as part of the company's restructuring toward pharmaceutical drug development, a business decision entirely unrelated to her scientific performance; I regard her transition to an academic fellowship as a natural and well-deserved next step, and one I fully expect her to succeed at.

I recommend Dr Ebenesersdóttir's application without reservation.

Yours sincerely,

**Kári Stefánsson**
CEO, deCODE Genetics

## 2.3 Magnitude and Importance of the Project's Contribution to Expected Scientific, Societal, and Economic Impacts

***[REORGANIZED — from Rannís Section A "Impact and originality" and background material, condensed for the MSCA magnitude/importance framing]***

This project will make the Icelandic horse one of the best genetically characterised horse breeds in the world. Many of the aims outlined above would not have been possible to achieve even five years ago, but have become feasible due to the lowering cost of generating WGS data, advances in ancient DNA methods, and the availability of a recombination map and haplotype reference panel for a diverse array of horses (Ahmed et al., under review).

Horses are one of many species that helped transform human societies, but were also transformed by humans through selective breeding. By comparing genomes from ancient and present-day Icelandic horses, it will be possible to document these changes in greater detail than for almost any other non-human animal species to date, informing future breeding and conservation strategies both for the Icelandic horse specifically and for other, more vulnerable breeds represented by fewer individuals.

The Icelandic horse is a living cultural and economic resource of considerable scale: approximately 80,000–100,000 registered horses in Iceland and over 100,000 internationally. Results will provide the first population-level baseline of genomic diversity from which current genetic health can be assessed, and will identify historical variants associated with heritable conditions relevant to modern breeding strategies.
